Pick–Nav–Reach

Measured simulation results: 5/15 deliveries and 13/15 picks meeting the local rubric checks.

Method

Antipodal proposals use observed surface points and normals. Damped Jacobian IK, sampled collision checks and bidirectional arm planning produce a pick trajectory. The controller closes the fingers and lifts through ordinary motor actions. It rotates and retracts the grasp for transport, then plans a fixed-heading holonomic base route with A* on a 10 cm lattice. Maze walls and the table are inflated by the robot and carried-object footprint. Exact segment tests reject corner cutting; a second check validates the full articulated geometry and payload. Bounded-acceleration commands stop at corners. Final IK corrections use the observed object offset, followed by 60 control steps at the goal.

Measurements

Delivery requires the object to remain within 10 cm of the goal throughout the final hold and completion within 15,000 physics steps. One control step advances five physics steps. Pick success additionally requires a first lift by 3,000 physics steps and at least 50 consecutive stable hold steps. Carry drift measures translation relative to the gripper; more than 9 cm or an object height below 45 cm aborts execution. Failures remain in the denominator.

Limits

These are development seeds, not hidden-test scores. The fixed-heading planner can reject routes feasible with base rotation. Carry geometry is rigid only during planning queries; execution relies on contact forces and can slip. Joint collision checks are discrete. The nominal friction coefficient used by the proposal filter is not a measured surface property. The supplied environment and planner assume one active PyBullet client per process. No fixed constraint attaches the object to the robot, and execution never teleports the robot or object.
